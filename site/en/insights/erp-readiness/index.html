PRACTICAL GUIDE

How to prepare for ELSAIDY business systems

Business-system readiness begins with clear processes, ownership and reliable data—not with software selection.

Business-system readiness begins with clear processes, ownership and reliable data—not with software selection.

01

Document current workflows and recurring errors.

02

Review accounts, inventory and staff permissions.

03

Start with a testable scope rather than launching everything at once.

04

Plan migration, training, recovery and user acceptance testing.

STEP 01

Map the workflow before choosing modules

Consider a contractor: a worker requests materials, a supervisor approves, purchasing raises an order and the storekeeper issues supplies to a project. Who owns each step? What happens after rejection? Clarifying these rules before implementation reduces expensive rework.

STEP 02

Clean the data before migration

Review customers, suppliers, staff, inventory and the chart of accounts with a named reviewer for each dataset. Importing incomplete or duplicate records does not repair them. Use a staging environment and reconcile balances before moving to production.

STEP 03

Prove one process first

Rather than launching every module simultaneously, pilot a clear workflow such as employee custody and expenses. Test creation, approval, settlement, cancellation and reporting. Expand only after business users have accepted the pilot.

STEP 04

Security and Saudi business requirements

Separate user permissions and approval roles. Establish backups and test an isolated recovery. Saudi e-invoicing obligations must be assessed for each company and integration stage; using business management software is not itself proof of compliance.

STEP 05

Training and measurement

Train each job role, track repeated issues and measure processing times, data accuracy and report usefulness. A successful implementation is one that people can operate reliably, not merely one with many screens.
